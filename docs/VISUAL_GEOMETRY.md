# Semantic Visual Geometry Validation

## Purpose and boundary

Prompt 25C added the measuring instrument. Prompt 25D hardened that instrument,
preserved its negative calibration fixtures, and corrected the production
content until the complete required matrix reached zero failures. The frozen
Prompt 25B-derived reports remain the before evidence; they are never
regenerated by the Prompt 25D acceptance command.

The validator is independent of texture packing. Anatomy records retain
`Owner.anatomy` or the permanent neutral `Owner.base_presentation`; removable
garments retain `Owner.clothing`; shoes retain `Owner.equipment`; hats and waist
pieces retain `Owner.accessory`. A shared PNG or GPU page does not merge those
identities.

## Representation and coordinate spaces

`visual_geometry.Geometry` declares only the landmarks meaningful for its typed
role. Current roles include torso, pelvis, limb segments, hands, feet, torso and
lower garments, sleeves, gloves, legwear, shoes, headwear, waist accessories,
hair, features, and an extensible custom role. It does not require a future
wing, tail, weapon, horn, animal part, or four-arm module to masquerade as a
human limb.

Landmarks are normalized inside the alpha-visible bounds, not the source
rectangle. The deterministic transform is:

```text
visible-relative landmark
  -> atlas source pixel
  -> production unit-quad local point
  -> SpriteRequest pivot and signed scale
  -> SpriteRequest rotation and world position
  -> production Camera2D acceptance pixels
```

`sprite_batch.localPointToWorld` is the CPU definition of the exact equation in
`sprite.hlsl::VSMain`; alpha sampling uses its checked inverse. Character and
wardrobe presenters append the exact `SpriteRequest` they submit to
`UnifiedRenderQueue`. The trace stores the character, owner, role, semantic
side, semantic/component ID, bound anatomy identity, region, view, and fit
contract. Side is obtained from the anatomy binding before submission and is
never inferred from scale sign.

The trace and report use fixed capacities: 256 records and 32,768 metrics.
Production submission remains allocation-free. PNG decoding, alpha-mask
sampling, and report formatting are audit-time work.

## Visible alpha geometry

`deriveAlphaGeometry` scans RGBA alpha at the documented threshold of 8 and
returns the opaque pixel bounds, dimensions, pixel count, and centroid.
Transparent authoring padding therefore cannot enlarge a body or garment for a
fit check. Coverage uses bounded inverse-transformed acceptance pixels and the
real atlas alpha, not quad intersection.

The validator reports target coverage, attachment pixels off the target, and
crossing onto the opposite semantic foot. Joint continuity separately reports
semantic endpoint error, minimum silhouette gap, and overlap depth projected
along the semantic joint axis at the deterministic 640 by 480,
82-pixel-per-metre camera.

## Contracts

Each wardrobe `VisualSpec` contains a bounded `FitContract`. The conventional
defaults retain 2 px registration, 5 degree axis/sole alignment, 0.95 to 1.15
footwear length, and 0.96 to 1.08 fitted spans. Coverage is explicitly the
declared target region, not every anatomy pixel. Garment-specific contracts
describe open halter backs, diagonal one-shoulder panels, puff clearance,
curved trouser seats, and split-skirt drape without weakening the generic
contract for unrelated garments.

Intermediate elbows, wrists, and ankles retain a maximum 5 px overlap at the
acceptance camera. Articulated knee caps permit 8 px through the deterministic
deep-bend/jump sweep. Pelvis bridges permit 15 px. Torso-owned deltoids retain
a 15 px nominal floor and may use at most 30 percent of the transformed torso
span so broad morphology can preserve the same normalized silhouette without
an unbounded pixel allowance. Controlled fixtures reject 8.1 px knees, 15.1 px
nominal roots, and 18.1 px overlap on a 60 px broad torso.

Checks include:

- shoe ankle, heel, toe, forward axis, sole axis, length, coverage, off-target
  pixels, opposite-foot crossing, and independent semantic bindings;
- torso neck, shoulder span, waist span, orientation, and visible coverage;
- lower-garment waist, hip, left/right leg openings, and coverage;
- sleeve, glove, legwear, and headwear attachment and orientation;
- sleeve, glove, and legwear visible coverage;
- endpoint and alpha-silhouette continuity at elbows, wrists, knees, and
  ankles;
- rendered limb spans relative to authoritative production binding spans;
- conventional-profile hand/forearm and foot/lower-leg ratios;
- optional neutral-front bilateral symmetry.

The expected relationships belong to the active conventional presentation
contract. There is no global human-proportion table.

## Pose and morphology matrix

The focused suite evaluates front, side-right, and side-left production
submissions; low, nominal, and high global build for both conventional male and
female; low/high shoulder width, hip width, upper-arm thickness, thigh
thickness, hand scale, and foot scale; four deterministic walk phases; a deep
knee bend; jump compression, rise, fall, and landing. Every production motion
sample is evaluated in side-right and side-left. These states come from the
existing character and visual-quality schedule.

## Decision record

- The shared sprite transform became a public production helper so tests and
  overlays cannot maintain a divergent transform formula.
- Trace collection is optional and fixed-size at the presenter/queue boundary.
  This preserves the renderer and keeps semantic identity available before
  mirroring and sorting.
- Alpha masks are decoded only for tests/audits/debug initialization. Keeping
  full CPU masks in every production character would violate the intended
  runtime ownership and memory boundary.
- Visible continuity uses acceptance-camera pixels because the earlier
  metre-only endpoint check could pass an obvious crop edge or excessive
  overlap.
- Golden comparison remains a change detector. Semantic metrics remain the
  authority for why a frame is wrong.
- Prompt 25B failure evidence is immutable under the Prompt 25D report route.
  The accepted content is evaluated separately, so before and after cannot be
  conflated.
- Style/view/semantic-region landmarks replaced inaccurate role-only pelvis and
  limb assumptions where the visible production pixels differed.
- Root, knee, and distal-joint continuity have distinct typed contracts. The
  generic distal limit was not loosened.
- Anatomy and wardrobe use one production quarter-turn morphology-axis helper;
  side-facing limb thickness cannot silently become sprite length in either
  submission path.
- Full Prompt 25D rationale and the no-test-gaming audit are in
  `PROMPT25D_DECISION_RECORD.md`.

## Prompt 27C anatomy-quality layer

`anatomy_quality.zig` extends the existing trace and alpha authority rather
than creating a screenshot-specific validator. Its data-driven contract is
selected by style and view, then applied independently to semantic left/right
records. It evaluates:

- neck-to-shoulder slope and alpha-derived shoulder curvature;
- shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee, and ankle parent/child width ratios;
- hand/forearm, arm/torso, head/torso, pelvis/torso, and thigh/shin ratios;
- dark transverse source-edge coverage at articulated interfaces;
- side-foot forward-axis error, front toe-out, and bilateral symmetry.

Pixel joint-overlap depth is the longest contiguous occupied run along the
semantic limb axis. Separated antialias islands are not treated as a solid
overlap spanning the empty projection rows between them. The existing
per-joint pixel thresholds are unchanged; controlled fixtures retain a
five-pixel ankle boundary and reject a six-pixel contiguous overlap.

The 102-case production matrix includes front, side-right, side-left,
relaxed/extended/bent limbs, deep knee bend, low/nominal/high global build,
and low/high supported morphology channels for both styles. A missing case or
nonfinite value is a report failure. Controlled scalar/mask fixtures prove
that square shoulders, gaps, blobs, disproportionate hands/arms, pinched
wrists, floating/excessive pelvis overlap, knee beads, ankle gaps, reversed
feet, invalid toe-out, and dark crop edges are rejected without a production
stable-ID exception.

Run:

```powershell
& '.\.deps\zig-0.16.0\zig.exe' build test-visual-geometry -j1
& '.\.deps\zig-0.16.0\zig.exe' build p27c-anatomy-report -j1 -- --output 'zig-out\p27c_evidence\final_semantic_report' --expect-pass
```

The final production report contains 102 cases, 2,450 trace records, 3,788
metrics, and zero failures. The frozen pre-correction report contains 817
failures and is never overwritten. The real D3D12 review package provides
clean body-only captures, a same-scale before/after sheet, and shoulder,
elbow/wrist, pelvis/hip, knee/ankle, and foot close-ups. Captures remain
review candidates until owner acceptance.

## VF-02 structural registration evidence

`RegistrationEvidence` is generated by the production registration solver
from the exact submitted `SpriteRequest`. It records binding mode, scale and
mirroring policies, source atlas landmarks, projected target identities,
source-local points, transformed world points, residuals, and the structural
threshold.

Front base feet and equipped shoes use `point_axis` structural contracts. The
ankle is the only positional target; `sole_back -> sole_front` is an
orientation axis. Heel, toe, sole-back, and sole-front remain transformed
evidence used by the independent contact ruler and are not independent target
endpoints. Base-foot policy retains explicit 16-degree male and 13-degree
female ground-plane toe-out, but front projection maps its signed lateral
component to horizontal view space and does not map physical depth to world Y.

The shoe's final request registers its alpha-derived ankle and authored sole
axis to the exact `PointAxisTarget` stored by the anatomy foot. It uses the
registered front foot's authored transverse scale, with no second rotation or
depth multiplier. Closed footwear explicitly suppresses covered base-foot
pixels while retaining their trace and transformed landmarks. This is why
production pixels, diagnostics, JSON, and Prompt 27B measure the same final
shoe transform without claiming full endpoint registration.

VF-01 schema version 3 and its overlay consume this evidence directly for
migrated components. The ordinary alpha/landmark resolver remains active for
silhouette, continuity, garment coverage, foot, and unmigrated-component
measurements. Structural residual success therefore cannot erase a source-art
or wardrobe-compatibility failure.

Historical Prompt 25D and Prompt 27C reports remain immutable. Fresh
current-art runs after VF-02 may fail because final pixels now follow
projected targets; those failures must be reported, not normalized by
changing landmark thresholds or substituting an older report.
