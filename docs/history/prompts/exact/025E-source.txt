Part V — Directional side presentation
13. Explicit reflection policy

Not every side-view visual is valid under generic reflection.

Define and document side-presentation policies equivalent to:

Mirror-safe

One canonical side visual may be reflected when gameplay facing changes.

Appropriate for visually symmetric or reflection-safe content.

Authored directional variants

The logical item provides separate side-left and side-right presentations.

Appropriate when front/back garment construction or asymmetric details make reflection visibly incorrect.

Mixed-fragment directional composition

Some fragments may reflect while others resolve to direction-specific art.

Appropriate for garments with:

a reusable mirrored body panel;
directional placket;
directional wrap;
one-sided bow;
asymmetric trim;
front/back-specific side detail.

Do not force separate left/right artwork for every garment.

Do not assume that body reflection automatically makes all equipment reflection correct.

14. Preserve semantic identity across facing changes

Changing gameplay facing must not:

change equipment identity;
swap outfit preset;
recreate equipment runtime state;
decode images;
create GPU resources;
reset deformation;
reset secondary motion;
swap anatomical left/right identity incorrectly.

Only the resolved presentation fragments and transforms may change.

Anatomical left/right identity must remain stable.

Part VII — Shoulder-slope-aware garment fit
19. Do not use a square torso hitbox as the shoulder contour

The physics collider is not the visual shoulder boundary.

Upper-garment fit must not assume:

flat horizontal shoulders;
square upper torso;
sleeves beginning at rectangular torso corners;
neck centered over a box with horizontal top edges.

Human shoulders slope downward from the neck toward the shoulder roots.

The character’s visual geometry must represent that fact.

20. Semantic shoulder geometry

Document and, where needed, expose visual-authoring landmarks equivalent to:

neck-base center;
left neck base;
right neck base;

right clavicle/upper-chest transition;
left shoulder seam point;
right shoulder seam point;
left shoulder root;
right shoulder root;
left armhole reference;
right armhole reference.

A compact contour representation may be used, such as:

landmark polyline;
bounded sampled contour;
semantic span plus slope;
another deterministic representation fitting the existing validator.

Do not introduce an unbounded arbitrary vector path system merely for this milestone.

21. Shoulder-slope fit contract

Upper garments such as:

blouses;
shirts;
jackets;
dresses;

armor tops;

must be validated against the actual visual shoulder geometry.

Validation should cover concepts equivalent to:

armhole registration;
upper-chest span;
torso waist span;
intended coverage;
clearance appropriate to fitted or loose design.

A garment must not pass solely because its axis-aligned rectangle covers the torso bounds.

22. Sleeves and shoulder transition

Sleeves remain topology-aware repeated visuals.

However, the torso garment and sleeve must agree at the shoulder/armhole transition.

Validate:

torso shoulder seam to anatomical shoulder;
sleeve proximal opening to shoulder/upper-arm region;
no large gap;
no excessive overlap blob;
correct body-relative ordering;
front and side presentation;
both gameplay facings where relevant;
morphology extremes.

Do not solve shoulder fit by making every sleeve or torso piece an oversized opaque patch.

23. Morphology-aware shoulder fit

Shoulder fit must remain valid across the bounded production morphology matrix.

At minimum inspect relevant extremes for:

shoulder width;
torso width;
chest depth;
global build;
upper-arm thickness.

The shoulder socket and garment shoulder geometry must respond only to semantically relevant morphology channels.

Do not move shoulder roots based on unrelated torso dimensions merely because they share a generic scale.

Do not require one sprite for every possible 0–100 morphology value.

Use the established landmark/deformation/fit-response architecture.
